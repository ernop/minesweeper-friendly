A Just Universe

A sealed pocket is a group of unknown cells whose possible mine arrangements all look identical from everywhere outside the group. No play outside it can tell you which internal cell is safe. When you bare-click into a certified sealed pocket, that entry is a forced coinflip — and you win it, always. Each one counts as one Justice survival. Every number already revealed and the total mine count remain true.

The key is outside the pocket: an internal safe click may reveal a number that resolves the rest, but there was no way to earn that information before entering. This is stricter than an ordinary “forced guess.” A corner 1 may force you to gamble somewhere, yet it does not force entry into one permanently unreadable pocket.

This first version certifies three exact, equal-odds structures: symmetric k-of-n pockets, alternating 50/50 pairs and chains, and a final sealed sea remnant. Before matching those shapes, the shared solver proves every cell fixed across all layouts consistent with the visible clues and total mine count. It never treats a work-limit result as proof and never estimates a qualification. An exotic ambiguity without one of these short structural proofs keeps ordinary Minesweeper behavior.

When Justice applies

2●A 2●B

The endgame 50/50. One mine left, two cells it could be in, and no number will ever see either cell (a wall of proven mines seals them off). The whole game comes down to a coin you were always going to have to flip. Justice. Bare-click either cell: the flip lands in your favor and counts once.

● = a cell proven to be a mine (it can never be opened, so it can never tell you anything). Gold outline = the cells the coin is between.

1A 13B 1●● 122

The sealed pair — even in mid-game. The 1 proves exactly one mine in A/B; the 3 and its neighbors prove the two mines below. Every number that touches A also touches B, and the only cells that could ever have told them apart are proven mines — so no future play, anywhere on the board, can ever break the tie. You will have to flip this coin sooner or later; flipping it now costs nothing. Justice.

a1b1c1d

The 50/50 chain. Each 1 links its two covered neighbors: exactly one mine in a–b, one in b–c, one in c–d. Only two layouts fit — mines at a&c, or mines at b&d — and the one-cell-high strip has no outside square that could distinguish them. The whole chain is one pocket: the first guaranteed click inside it may show a number that resolves the rest. Justice.

Symmetric sealed pockets with more than two layouts count too: in a certified 1-in-3 nook, for example, every cell has the same 1-in-3 mine risk, so a bare click into any of them receives Justice.

When Justice does NOT apply

1

The early-game open guess. A lone corner 1 and a sea of unknowns: you do have to guess something — but no particular cell is forced on you. Any cell you pick could, in some other line of play, have been figured out from its neighbors first. That is the ordinary price of exploring a random board. Real risk. (Clicking next to the 1 at worse odds than the open field is also, still, real risk.)

1A 13B 1●● 122

The pair that could still be solved. Same shape as the sealed pair — but here the board keeps going to the right, and that flank is unexplored, not proven. Open those cells later (the right-hand 2 already proves the two beside it are safe) and their numbers begin to tell A from B. Information about this pair is still purchasable, so it is not a sealed coin yet. Real risk.

121 xz

The knowable mine — and every chord. 1-2-1: the numbers already prove x and z are mines and the middle is safe. Clicking a provable mine is a mistake. Chording is also categorically outside this rule: a flag is your claim, not information supplied by the board, and a wrong flag may kill even when the surrounding uncertainty was unavoidable. No Justice.

In one sentence: when you bare-click into a certified pocket that no outside clue can ever resolve, that entry is guaranteed safe.

Each qualifying entry adds one to the game's Justice count. When the game ends, the count appears once to the board's right: you won a forced coinflip. The setting is fixed at the first reveal and cannot change until the game ends; switch it off before starting for ordinary Minesweeper.
