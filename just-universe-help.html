A Just Universe

You never lose to a coin the universe minted specifically for you. When the game corners you into a spot that no possible way of playing could ever have figured out — and you take the best odds it offers — the mine "just so happens" to be in the other cell. Everything else about the board stays an honest, ordinary random board: ordinary guesses keep their ordinary risk, and every number you ever saw stays true.

The test is strict, and it is about the place, not the moment: a spot is truly forced only when every winning line of play — in any order, with any skill — must eventually click into that exact sealed pocket while it is still a mystery. If any information about it could ever have been earned, anywhere, at any price, the risk is real.

When it saves you

2●A 2●B

The endgame 50/50. One mine left, two cells it could be in, and no number will ever see either cell (a wall of proven mines seals them off). The whole game comes down to a coin you were always going to have to flip. Saved. Click either cell — they are exactly equal — and you win it.

● = a cell proven to be a mine (it can never be opened, so it can never tell you anything). Gold outline = the cells the coin is between.

1A 13B 1●● 122

The sealed pair — even in mid-game. The 1 proves exactly one mine in A/B; the 3 and its neighbors prove the two mines below. Every number that touches A also touches B, and the only cells that could ever have told them apart are proven mines — so no future play, anywhere on the board, can ever break the tie. You will have to flip this coin sooner or later; flipping it now costs nothing. Saved.

a1 1b1 1c1 1d

The 50/50 chain. Each 1 links two neighbors: exactly one mine in a–b, one in b–c, one in c–d. Only two layouts fit — mines at a&c, or mines at b&d — and when the chain's flanks are sealed (fragment shown; the dimmed cells stand for revealed or proven-mine surroundings), nothing can ever say which. The whole chain is one coin: survive one click inside it and every number it shows resolves the rest. Saved.

Sealed pockets with more than two layouts (a 1-in-3 nook, say) count too — as long as you enter at the pocket's best odds. Take a worse cell inside the pocket when a better one existed and the risk is yours.

When it does NOT save you

1

The early-game open guess. A lone corner 1 and a sea of unknowns: you do have to guess something — but no particular cell is forced on you. Any cell you pick could, in some other line of play, have been figured out from its neighbors first. That is the ordinary price of exploring a random board. Real risk. (Clicking next to the 1 at worse odds than the open field is also, still, real risk.)

1A 13B 1●● 1

The pair that could still be solved. Same shape as the sealed pair — but the bottom flank is unexplored, not proven. Open those cells later and their numbers would tell A from B. Information about this pair is still purchasable, so it is not a sealed coin yet. Real risk.

121 xz

The knowable mine. 1-2-1: the numbers already prove x and z are mines and the middle is safe. Clicking a provable mine — or chording on a wrong flag the numbers could have caught — is a mistake, not a coin. Real risk.

In one sentence: you can lose to your own choices and to the open field's ordinary odds, but never to a sealed pocket that no amount of play could ever have read — walk into one of those at its best odds and you "just so happen" to win.

A save is invisible while you play (that's the point). When the game ends you get one big JUSTICE stamp per save, and the count is stored on the game's record (the "Justice" row in the stats). Switch the setting off for the fully classic experience: the same situations simply kill you.
