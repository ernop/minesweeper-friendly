A Just Universe

When you click a square in a guess that nothing else on the board could ever settle, that square is guaranteed safe. Such a guess is a group of unknown squares whose possible mine layouts all show the same numbers everywhere outside the group, so nothing you could open first would tell you which of its squares is safe. A 50/50 boxed in by mines or the board's edge is the common case. Every number already shown and the total mine count stay true.

The test is what the rest of the board could tell you. A safe click inside the group may show a number that settles the rest of it, but there was no way to learn that before clicking. This is stricter than an ordinary “forced guess”: a corner 1 may force you to gamble somewhere, yet it does not force you into one particular guess that can never be settled.

What it covers: 50/50s, chains of 50/50s, groups where every arrangement of their mines is possible (like 1 mine among 3 squares), and the last squares no number touches once the mine count fixes how many mines they hold. Each guess is judged on its own, even when a number it shares links it to other unknown squares. Before deciding, the game works out everything the numbers and the mine count prove. A guess that matches none of these exact patterns plays as ordinary Minesweeper.

The endgame 50/50. One mine left, two squares it could be in, and no number will ever see either square: proven mines wall them off. The whole game comes down to a coin you were always going to have to flip. Guaranteed safe. Click either square: the flip goes your way and counts once.

The boxed-in pair, even mid-game. The 1 proves exactly one mine in A/B; the 3 and its neighbors prove the two mines below. Every number that touches A also touches B, and the only squares that could ever have told them apart are proven mines, so no later play anywhere on the board can break the tie. You will have to flip this coin sooner or later; flipping it now costs nothing. Guaranteed safe.

●●●● A5C3 B2D1

Two 50/50s that share a number. The 1 sees only C and D: one mine there. The 2 sees A, B, C, and D, so the other mine is in A/B. The 2 and the 5 tie the two pairs together, but neither pair can ever be settled: every number sees A and B alike and C and D alike, and every other square around them is a proven mine. These are two separate coins. Guaranteed safe, each of them.

a1b1c1d

The 50/50 chain. Each 1 links its two covered neighbors: exactly one mine in a–b, one in b–c, one in c–d. Only two layouts fit, mines at a and c or mines at b and d, and the one-square-high strip has no other square that could tell them apart. The whole chain is one guess: the first click inside it may show a number that settles the rest. Guaranteed safe.

Groups with more than two layouts count too: when 1 mine sits among 3 squares that every number sees alike, each square has the same 1-in-3 risk, and a click on any of them is guaranteed safe.

Not covered

1

The early-game open guess. A lone corner 1 and a field of unknowns: you do have to guess something, but no particular square is forced on you. Any square you pick could, in some other line of play, have been worked out from its neighbors first. That is the ordinary price of exploring a random board. Real risk. (Clicking next to the 1 at worse odds than the open field is also real risk.)

The pair that could still be settled. Same shape as the boxed-in pair, but here the board goes on to the right, and those squares are unopened, not proven mines. Open them later (the right-hand 2 already proves the two beside it are safe) and their numbers begin to tell A from B. You can still learn something about this pair, so it is not a coin yet. 121 xz

The knowable mine, and every chord. 1-2-1: the numbers already prove x and z are mines and the middle is safe. Clicking a provable mine is a mistake. Chords are never covered: a flag is your claim, not something the board told you, and a wrong flag can kill even when the uncertainty around it was unavoidable. Not covered.

In one sentence: when you click a square in a guess that nothing else on the board could ever settle, that square is guaranteed safe.

Each such click counts once. When the game ends, the count appears once to the board's right: you won a forced coinflip. The setting is fixed at the first reveal and cannot change until the game ends; switch it off before starting for ordinary Minesweeper.
