The plan

Your finish time equals total inputs × seconds per input, so 3BV/s (board work cleared per second, where 3BV is the minimum left clicks needed to clear the board) equals IOE (Index of Efficiency: 3BV divided by all clicks you made, including no-op clicks that changed nothing) divided by seconds per input. Empirical comparison of your traces against sub-60-second Expert replays shows both factors matter: sub-60 players spend about a fifth fewer clicks per board and about 40% less time per click, driven by five measured habits below. Rules from earlier stages stay on as you advance.

Every session

  • Play 45–60 minutes on most days. Split longer practice into two sessions hours apart: gains per sitting level off within an hour, and distributed practice consolidates pattern memory faster.
  • Warm up for 5 minutes on sub-board skill drills or Intermediate with your current stage's focus, then play Expert in 10-minute blocks with one specific habit in focus per block.
  • Open in a corner. If the corner shows a number, restart with Space instead of guessing: a corner click opens a zero about half the time by chance, and a restart costs one second.
  • Keep mouse, sensitivity, and zoom fixed. When one changes, tag a player state so before and after stay comparable.
  • Stop when a block drops clearly below your best block of the session, or after two avoidable losses in a row past 20 seconds. Do not wait until you feel tired: trace data shows rhythm degrades before fatigue is consciously felt.
  • Review the mentation replay inspector at the end of each session and mark whether the model's claims on your slowest moves match what you were thinking.

1. Stop wasting inputs

  • Cut habit no-ops: click a number to chord only when all of its mines are flagged and covered neighbors remain. Never click a number to test whether it is ready.
  • Use the 1.5-click (202 ms press-to-press): when a number is one flag short, press right on the mine, keep holding right as you slide onto the number, and click left to chord in one continuous motion. Practice in the Last-flag drill and Coach: No-waste.
  • Count chord payoff before flagging: flag and chord only when the chord opens more squares than the flags it needs (1 flag for 2+ safe squares, 2 flags for 3+). When a chord opens only 1 square (especially along an opening rim), left-click that safe square directly.
  • Finish the endgame directly (no pointless flags): when 5 or fewer safe squares remain on the board, stop placing flags that open zero chords or 1-square chords and left-click the remaining safe squares directly to win. Practice in the Endgame direct-finish drill.
  • Turn on IOE, no-op clicks, and unused flag share in the game data column to monitor every win against your history.

2. Finish the runs you start

  • Recognize pre-death flow breakdown: across late Expert losses (games lasting 20 seconds or more), your press-to-press pace slows across the final 5 moves before death while cross-board jumps and no-op clicks surge. When you hit two no-ops in a row or stall past 20 seconds, pause your hand and scan the board with your eyes before clicking.
  • Check for ready 4, 5, 6, and 7 numbers: partially-flagged high numbers whose remaining covered neighbors are all mines are easy to overlook mid-board. Scan for them before taking any cross-board jump or guess, and drill them in the Ready 4/5/6/7 pop-out drill.
  • Never guess while a safe move exists: before any unproven click, scan every open border for a simple single-number counting move. Practice in Coach: Scan shield and 5 minutes a day of Endgame drill.
  • When you spot an overflagged or contradicted number, remove the wrong flag immediately before chording anywhere near it.

3. Read and move faster

  • Eyes-first reading (stop slow cursor reading): read clue numbers with your eyes before moving the mouse so your cursor travels in a straight line (path ratio near 1.0–1.2× straight-line distance) instead of looping slowly over open clues while thinking. Practice in the Eyes-first anti-drift drill.
  • Bail first on slow shapes when easy moves wait: fresh overlap deductions (interior 1-2, straight-edge 1-2-1, overlap chains, 1-3-1 corners) take 700–940 ms when newly uncovered, whereas simple counting moves take 210–370 ms. Whenever 2 or more simple counting moves are open elsewhere, clear them first; often they open the slow area from behind. Practice in Coach: Early bail and the Bail-vs-solve shape drill.
  • Eyes-ahead lookahead on cross-board jumps: as you click the final square of a local area, shift your gaze to the next active area before releasing the button so your cursor departs within 45 ms with zero mid-flight detours. Practice in the Two-area eyes-ahead transition drill.
  • Automate cluttered no-flag counting and 1-2 / 1-2-1 chunks: train whole 2–4-click bursts inside cluttered sub-boards on the problems & skill drills page so fresh recognition drops toward queued execution speed.

4. Win under 60 seconds

  • The first win under 60 seconds usually arrives on a favorable-3BV board (around 150 3BV or lower, roughly one Expert board in nine) once your median win is near 70 seconds; at Stage 3's 2.80 3BV/s target it becomes routine. Keep every rule active and finish clean runs.
